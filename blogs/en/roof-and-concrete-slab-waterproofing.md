# Roof & Concrete Slab Waterproofing in KL and Selangor

**Question:** Why does my concrete roof slab or balcony keep leaking even after patching?

**Answer:** Because concrete cracks and moves. Rainwater enters through shrinkage cracks, construction joints and failed seams, then travels inside the slab before showing up as a ceiling stain metres away from the entry point. Patching the stain-side never works for long. Durable slab waterproofing means treating the exposed top side as one continuous system: repairing cracks and joints, correcting ponding where possible, detailing every upstand and penetration, and applying a continuous membrane — an elastomeric UV-resistant acrylic for exposed areas or a torch-on bituminous membrane for heavy-duty protection.

## Where Concrete Slabs Leak

- **Flat RC roofs** of terrace houses, shops and intermediate extensions.
- **Balconies and open decks** exposed to daily rain and standing water.
- **Planter boxes** built against the slab edge.
- **Bathrooms on upper floors**, where the slab carries constant moisture — usually handled as a [wet-area waterproofing](/services/waterproofing/bathroom-and-wet-area-waterproofing) scope instead.
- **Car porch roofs** and drying-yard slabs over extensions.

## The Two Main Membrane Systems

| Aspect | Elastomeric acrylic membrane | Torch-on bituminous membrane |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Liquid-applied coating, reinforced with mesh at joints | Pre-formed rolls torched and fused onto the slab |
| UV exposure | Designed for exposed, sun-facing surfaces | Usually protected by screed, tiles or coating |
| Seam risk | Seamless when done properly | Seams must be fully fused — poor overlaps leak |
| Crack-bridging | Flexible, bridges hairline movement | Strong, but detailing at cracks matters more |
| Best for | Exposed balconies, flat roofs, planter exteriors | Flat roofs, decks under screed/tiles, high-traffic surfaces |

The published sub-service description covers heavy-duty elastomeric UV-resistant acrylic and torch-on bituminous membranes; the right system is chosen after inspecting the slab's exposure, usage and existing finish.

## What a Proper Slab Waterproofing Job Includes

1. **Leak tracing** — the stain inside the house rarely sits under the entry point; the top side is inspected for cracks, blisters, failed joints and hollow patches.
2. **Surface preparation** — the slab is cleaned of dust, algae, loose coating and laitance; a membrane only bonds to a clean, sound surface.
3. **Crack and joint repair** — cracks are opened, filled and reinforced; failed sealant at joints is cut out and redone. Active structural cracks may be injected first — see [PU injection grouting](/services/waterproofing/pu-high-pressure-injection-grouting).
4. **Ponding correction** — low spots that hold rainwater shorten every membrane's life; levelling mortar or re-screeding corrects them where feasible.
5. **Detailing** — corners, upstands, pipe penetrations, drain outlets and door thresholds get reinforced treatment because that is where 90% of failures begin.
6. **Membrane application** — coating systems go on in multiple coats to the specified thickness; torch-on membranes are rolled, torched and fused with sealed overlaps.
7. **Flood or spray testing** — the finished area is tested before closing up or handing over.

## Detailing Decides Everything

Slab waterproofing rarely fails in the middle of a flat area. It fails at:

- **Upstands and parapets** — the membrane must turn up the wall and terminate correctly under a counter-flashing or reglet.
- **Drain outlets** — the throat needs proper priming and membrane continuity into the drain body.
- **Pipe penetrations** — rainwater tank lines and aircond drains through the slab need sealed collars.
- **Door thresholds and slab edges** — the termination must stop water tracking sideways under the membrane.
- **Old-to-new joints** — where an extension slab meets the original house, movement never stops; the joint gets a flexible system, not rigid filler.

## Maintenance After Waterproofing

- Keep drains and scuppers clear of leaves — ponding water is the number one membrane killer.
- Don't hack, drill or nail into a waterproofed slab without resealing the penetration.
- Inspect yearly before the monsoon: look for blisters, open seams and cracked sealant.
- Re-top exposed acrylic systems when the manufacturer-rated life is reached — recoating early is far cheaper than re-leaking later.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you waterproof a slab while it is still leaking from rain yesterday?
The slab must be reasonably dry before membranes bond properly. Work is scheduled around the weather, and in the monsoon season temporary mitigation may be done first, with permanent application in a dry window.

### Should I waterproof over tiles on my balcony?
Coating over tiles is usually a short-term compromise — trapped moisture under tiles still migrates. Durable balcony waterproofing normally means removing tiles, waterproofing the bare slab with proper detailing, and retiling on top. See the [balcony leak problem guide](/problems/balcony-leak-condo) for the decision factors.

### How long does slab waterproofing take to complete?
It depends on the area and system: a small flat roof can be prepared and coated within days, while torch-on jobs with screed and protection layers take longer. Curing time between coats is not optional — rushing coats is how pinholes happen.
