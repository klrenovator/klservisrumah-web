# Lighting Point & Downlight Installation in KL and Selangor

**Question:** Can I add new lights to an existing ceiling without rewiring the whole house?

**Answer:** Yes — that is exactly what adding a lighting point is. An electrician cuts the desired hole in the plaster ceiling, pulls a new cable from a nearby lighting circuit through the ceiling space, installs a proper junction and switch control, then the opening is patched and repainted. The whole-house wiring stays untouched; only the branch feeding the new point is added. The two things that must be planned well are the switching — which switch controls the new light — and the patch — so the ceiling looks like the light was always there.

## When Homeowners Add Lighting Points

- **Dark rooms or corridors** — the old single-centre point leaves shadows everywhere.
- **Downlight conversion** — replacing one central fitting with evenly-spaced recessed LEDs.
- **Pendant lights** — a new dining or island pendant needs a point directly above the table.
- **Feature and cove lighting** — L-box and cove LED installations each need their own feed and switching.
- **Renovation additions** — new rooms, extended kitchens and converted storerooms need fresh points.

## How a New Point Is Added

1. **Mark the positions** — downlights are spaced for even coverage against walls; pendants are centred over the furniture, not the room.
2. **Open the ceiling** — a clean hole is cut at each point; access openings stay only where wiring requires them.
3. **Pull the cable** — a new cable runs from the nearest suitable lighting circuit or junction in the ceiling space (or up to the roof space in landed homes) to each new point.
4. **Wire the control** — points are connected to the planned switch group: one switch per zone per effect, so pendants, downlights and cove strips switch independently.
5. **Mount and terminate** — holders/connectors fitted, ready for the chosen fixtures.
6. **Patch and paint** — ceiling openings and any cable channel are skimmed and repainted to blend in.

## Planning the Switching

The most common regret in added lighting is bad switch planning. Decide before cutting:

- **Zones:** living downlights rarely belong on the same switch as the dining pendant.
- **Dimming:** dimmable LED points need compatible dimmer switches — plan them at this stage with the point, not after.
- **Two-way switching:** staircases and long corridors should be switchable from both ends.
- **Existing switch capacity:** adding four points onto a single old 1-gang switch plate usually means a new switch location anyway — it is cheap to add while the ceiling is open.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to repaint the whole ceiling after adding points?
No — only the patched openings and any cable channels are skimmed and blended. In most cases the patch is invisible after paint; very old or textured ceilings may need a wider repaint area to blend perfectly.

### Can new points work with my existing switch?
Sometimes — a new point can join an existing lighting circuit and switch, but most homeowners adding downlights want independent switching per zone. The electrician checks circuit load and plans the groups with you before cutting.

### How many downlights does a room need?
A common planning approach is spacing downlights roughly at half the ceiling height apart, and keeping them away from walls to avoid scallop shadows — but fixture wattage, room use and reflectance change the real number.
