# Commercial Shoplot Renovation in KL and Selangor

**Question:** What should a tenant plan before renovating a rented shoplot?

**Answer:** Three things: the landlord's renovation conditions, the licence-driven requirements for your trade, and a scope that separates fast-track essentials from nice-to-haves. ## What a Shoplot Fit-Out Typically Covers

- **Layout and partitions** — retail floor vs back-of-house, storage, office and toilets.
- **Electrical upgrades** — review of the incoming supply and DB for your equipment load, additional power points, display lighting and emergency lighting points.
- **Plumbing** — sink and water points; F&B units need far more: kitchen sinks, greasier waste considerations, floor traps and wash points planned with the layout.
- **Ceilings** — plaster ceiling, exposed-ceiling spraying, or L-box lighting features.
- **Flooring** — commercial-grade vinyl, tiles or epoxy chosen for foot traffic and cleaning, not just looks.
- **Shopfront and signage readiness** — signage panels, pelmet/awning base and wiring provisions ready for your signboard contractor; licences for signboards are applied to the local council.
- **Painting and finishing** — brand colours, washable commercial-grade finishes.

## F&B Units Are a Different Job

Food and beverage shops are the most demanding shoplot fit-outs because of the trade laws and services behind them:

- **Kitchen and service layout** dictates every water, drainage and exhaust point — it must be finalised before rough-in starts.
- **Grease and waste** flows need proper floor traps and maintenance access, not just a sink; kitchens without serviceable drainage block within months.
- **Ventilation and exhaust** provisions are planned alongside the ceiling design, not retrofitted into a finished ceiling.
- **Fire and licensing** requirements from the local council and Bomba apply to commercial premises — plan them into the timeline, not after it.

An experienced fit-out crew asks for your equipment list and seating plan before quoting; anyone who quotes a restaurant fit-out without them is guessing.

## Working Around Landlord and Mall Conditions

- **Hoarding and permits** — malls and managed shoplot strips require work permits, approved drawings and hoarding standards before day one.
- **Delivery and debris routes** — designated only; after-hours rules may apply in malls similar to condos.
- **Utilities** — confirm the incoming electrical capacity early; upgrading supply takes lead time.
- **Make-good clause** — know what must be reinstated at lease end; it affects whether you hack floors and ceilings or overlay them.

## The Fast-Track Staging Order

1. **Approval and site measure** — tenancy conditions, drawings, signage application started.
2. **Rough-in** — partitions framed, electrical and plumbing first-fix, exhaust provisions.
3. **Close-up** — ceiling, board, skim; floor substrate prepared.
4. **Finishes** — flooring, painting, glass/shopfront works.
5. **Second-fix electrical** — sockets, lighting, testing.
6. **Handover** — snag list, cleaning, keys on the agreed opening date.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How early should I engage a contractor before getting the keys?
As soon as the tenancy is confirmed. Measuring, drawing, landlord submissions and signage applications run best in parallel with your fitting-out period — they are the long-lead items, not the actual construction.

### Can you work within mall renovation rules?
Mall jobs follow each mall's contractor registration, deposit, hoarding and after-hours rules. Share the mall's fit-out guideline document at the start so the schedule and compliance costs are built into the quote.

### Do you handle the signboard too?
The renovation scope includes signage readiness — the panel/board base, wiring and mounting provisions. Artwork design, fabrication and the council signboard licence are normally handled by your signage supplier with the spot prepared for them.
