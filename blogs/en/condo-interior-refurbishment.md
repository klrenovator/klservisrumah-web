# Condo Interior Refurbishment in KL and Selangor

**Question:** What makes condo renovation different from renovating a landed house?

**Answer:** The building management. A condo unit sits inside a shared structure, so almost every meaningful interior work — hacking, drilling, tiling, rewiring — requires renovation approval from the management office before work begins. There are work-hour restrictions (no noisy work outside permitted hours), lift bookings for materials, mandatory lift and corridor protection, contractor deposits that are forfeited if common areas are damaged, and rules about wet areas. A condo refurbishment that ignores these gets stopped mid-project; one planned around them runs on schedule.

## What a Condo Interior Refurbishment Covers

A full interior rebuild typically combines some or all of:

- **Kitchen** — cabinets, countertops, sink, tiling and appliance points.
- **Bathrooms** — retiling or no-hack waterproofing refresh, sanitaryware, shower screen.
- **Flooring** — SPC/laminate overlay or tile replacement throughout.
- **Ceilings** — plaster ceiling repairs, L-box and lighting integration.
- **Painting** — full unit repaint after repairs.
- **Electrical** — additional power points, lighting points, DB review.
- **Doors and carpentry** — bedroom doors, built-in wardrobes, TV console.

Demolition comes first where layouts or finishes are changing; debris disposal runs through the booked lift with proper protection, not through common corridors.

## The Condo Approval Sequence

1. **Obtain management requirements** — forms, drawings needed, deposit amount, permitted working hours.
2. **Draw up the scope** — layout changes, wet areas affected, and any item touching common property (windows, grille, facade — usually restricted or uniform-spec).
3. **Submit and get approval** — works cannot start before this; approvals typically take days to a few weeks depending on the building.
4. **Pay the renovation deposit** — refundable, held against corridor lift damage and waste rules.
5. **Book the lift and protect common areas** — lift padding, corridor floor protection, designated waste route.

## Work-Hour Compliance in Practice

Management rules usually restrict hacking, drilling and heavy work to defined weekday hours, with lighter work sometimes allowed on Saturdays and no work on Sundays or public holidays. This changes scheduling fundamentally:

- Noisy stages (hacking, tile cutting, drilling) are batched into permitted hours.
- Quiet stages (painting, carpentry assembly, silicone work) fill the remaining time.
- Material deliveries must align with lift bookings, not with when the lorry happens to arrive.
- The published condo refurbishment scope explicitly includes management work-hour compliance and lift protection — non-compliance risks stop-work orders and lost deposits.

## Wet Areas: The Untouchable Rule

Bathroom and wet-area positions in a condo almost never move, because waterproofing continuity and shared drainage runs belong to the building system. A sensible plan refreshes within the existing wet footprint: new tiles, waterproofing refresh, sanitaryware and screens — without hacking that disturbs the unit below's ceiling. Any leak from your renovation into the unit below becomes your liability, which is why wet-area works are sequenced with pond testing before retiling.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a condo refurbishment usually take?
Timing depends on scope and work-hour limits rather than size alone. Painting-and-flooring refreshes move quickly; full kitchen-and-bathroom rebuilds under 5-hour work windows take proportionally longer than the same job in a landed house. A staged schedule is agreed before work begins.

### Can I change the windows, grille or front door?
Usually with restrictions. Facade-facing elements tend to be governed by uniform building specifications — the management has the final word, and approvals must be in writing before ordering anything.

### What if my renovation causes a leak to the unit below?
You are liable for the damage. This is why wet-area work includes pond testing before finishes, and why a contractor who sequences waterproofing properly matters more in a condo than anywhere else. Report any wet-area work to the management as required.
