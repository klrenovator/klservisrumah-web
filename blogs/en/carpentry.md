## What “custom carpentry” means on a quotation

Custom joinery is not the same as assembling a flat-pack wardrobe on site. A proper job is:

1. Measured on site (including ceiling drop, air-cond trunking and skirting).
2. Drawn and quoted with carcass material, door finish and hardware named.
3. Cut and edged in a workshop.
4. Installed, scribed to the wall and ceiling, then adjusted.

If someone is cutting raw boards in your living room with no edge banding and no drawing, that is site carpentry of a different standard.

## Plywood vs MDF for built-ins

| | Plywood | MDF |
|---|---|---|
| Best use | Wardrobe carcass, shelves that take weight, anything you may refit later | Painted door panels, feature-wall panelling, flat decorative faces |
| Moisture | Better than MDF if a window sweats or a cup spills | Swells if it takes water — avoid for wet or window-side carcasses |
| Screws | Holds hinges and rails well | Fine for light panels; hinges need proper inserts |
| Finish | Usually laminated or veneered | Usually spray-painted or wrapped |

Most KL wardrobes we build use a **plywood carcass** with laminate or painted doors. Feature walls often mix MDF panelling on a plywood or GI frame. The written quote should say which is which.

## When you need built-in carpentry

- A bedroom has a 600–700 mm recess that a shop wardrobe cannot fill.
- You want the wardrobe to hit the ceiling so clothes do not collect dust on top.
- The TV wall has a mess of decoder, fibre and plug points.
- The entrance is too narrow for a deep shoe rack.
- A condo study needs a desk that does not waste the only window wall.

If a wardrobe door already refuses to close, that can be hinge sag, a swollen door, or a racked carcass. See [wardrobe door not closing](/problems/wardrobe-door-not-closing) before you assume you need a full rebuild.

## How a carpentry job runs

1. **Site visit and measurement** — laser or tape on width, height, depth; note AC, beams and sockets.
2. **Design and quotation** — internals (drawers vs hanging), door style (swing, sliding, walk-in), finish and hardware.
3. **Workshop manufacturing** — typically **2–3 weeks** after design approval.
4. **On-site installation** — 1–2 days for a typical bedroom wardrobe; longer for a full TV wall.
5. **Alignment and handover** — doors planed to the reveal, soft-close tuned, handles fitted, site swept.

Condo work follows management hours, lift padding and debris rules. We regularly install in high-rises as well as landed houses.

## What we check before we cut boards

- Wall plumb and floor level — sliding doors fail first on a sloped floor.
- Whether the wall is brick, concrete or gypsum — fixings change.
- Hidden pipes and cables before we chase or drill a TV wall. For mounting only a TV, [heavy TV wall mounting](/services/handyman/heavy-tv-wall-mounting) may be enough.
- Door swing versus bed position and air-cond blow.
- Whether you need extra [electrical](/services/electrical) points inside the wardrobe or under the desk.

## Common mistakes

- Measuring width but forgetting the ceiling bulkhead, so doors cannot open.
- Using MDF shelves for folded jeans in a humid room with no edge seal.
- Building a TV cabinet with no rear cable void, then drilling the back panel later.
- Choosing sliding doors on a run that is too shallow for the track.
- Skipping soft-close on heavy wardrobe doors.
