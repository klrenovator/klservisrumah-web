# Water Heater & Pump Installation in KL and Selangor

**Question:** Why is my shower weak even though the water heater is working?

**Answer:** In many KL and Selangor homes — especially double-storey terrace houses fed by a roof tank — the upstairs shower depends on gravity pressure, which is often too low for an instant water heater to sense and stay on. The heater needs minimum flow to activate; below it, the water runs lukewarm or the unit cuts out mid-shower. A correctly sized pressure booster pump, installed together with or ahead of the heater, is the usual fix — but the pump location and pipe layout must be right, or the pump adds noise without adding pressure.

## When a Home Needs a Booster Pump

- Upstairs shower pressure is weak while downstairs taps are strong.
- An instant water heater flickers off or will not heat fully because flow is below its activation threshold.
- A newly added bathroom or rain shower needs more flow than gravity supply delivers.
- The whole house runs on a gravity tank system and fixtures were upgraded over the years.
- You are installing a storage water heater and want consistent pressure at every outlet.

In high-rise condos, pressure is normally managed by the building's own system, so individual pump installation is rarely allowed or needed — check with management first.

## Heater Types at a Glance

| Type | How it works | Suits | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instant (tankless) | Heats water as it flows | Single bathrooms, low usage points | Minimum inlet pressure/flow to activate |
| Storage tank | Heats and stores a set volume | Families, multiple bathrooms | Space, support for tank weight, dedicated electrical supply |
| Heat pump | Extracts heat from ambient air to heat a tank | Landed homes with high hot-water usage | Outdoor space, tank location, higher upfront cost |

A pump can be paired with any of these when inlet pressure is insufficient — for instant heaters it is often the difference between a unit that works and one that frustrates.

## How the Installation Works

1. **Pressure and flow check** — measure inlet pressure at the heater point and at key outlets to confirm a pump is actually needed and where.
2. **Pump selection** — size the pump to the house's demand (number of bathrooms running at once) rather than simply picking the most powerful unit; oversized pumps cycle noisily and stress old pipes.
3. **Pump placement** — typically after the storage tank outlet or on the incoming line, as configured for the house layout.
4. **Heater mounting and plumbing** — secure wall mounting or tank support, correct hot/cold connections, isolation valves for future servicing.
5. **Electrical supply** — instant heaters and storage heaters need a dedicated power point with proper protection; that is an electrical scope, see [aircond, water heater & oven point installation](/services/electrical/aircond-water-heater-and-oven-point). Water pump wiring is also routed to a protected supply.
6. **Commissioning** — run the system at full demand: all targeted outlets together, check heater activation, temperature stability and pump noise.

## Safety Checks That Should Never Be Skipped

- A water heater is a high-load appliance next to water every day — it must have its own protected circuit, correct cable size and working earth leakage protection (RCCB).
- The heater earthing must be verified, not assumed.
- Storage tanks need proper support; a full tank is heavy and must be anchored per the manufacturer's requirements.
- An RCCB that trips after installing a new heater is a warning, not a nuisance — see [RCCB keeps tripping](/problems/rccb-tripping-kl).

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Buying an instant heater first and discovering the house pressure is too low for it afterwards.
- Fitting a pump on the wrong side of the system so it pressurises only one fixture.
- Oversizing the pump "to be safe" — noise, pipe stress and wasted electricity.
- Running a new high-power heater from a shared general-purpose socket circuit.
- Ignoring a new RCCB trip after installation instead of having the circuit checked.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will a pump fix an instant heater that keeps cutting out?
If the cut-outs are caused by flow dropping below the heater's activation threshold, yes — a correctly positioned booster pump usually solves it. If the outlet pressure is already adequate, the fault may be inside the heater (element, flow sensor or thermostat), which is a repair case instead.

### Can I add a pump to my condo unit?
Usually no — condo water pressure is controlled by the building management's system and owners are not allowed to tap a private pump into it. Report persistent low pressure to the management office; the [water pressure troubleshooter](/tools/water-pressure-troubleshooter) helps you document the symptom clearly.

### Should the pump be installed before or after the water tank?
Placement depends on your house layout and what you want to pressurise — whole-house boosting normally goes on the main line after the tank, while single-bathroom solutions are positioned to feed that bathroom's heater line. The installer confirms the correct position after checking your pipe layout.
