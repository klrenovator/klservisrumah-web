An instant heater is the most popular choice in Malaysian homes because it gives hot water immediately without a storage tank. This guide covers how it works, what installation involves, and what to check before you buy.

## How an Instant Water Heater Works

An instant (single-point) heater is mounted above the shower or basin. Cold water flows through the unit, where a 3.5kW–4.5kW heating element heats it on the way to the showerhead. There is no tank, so hot water never runs out — but the unit serves only one point.

### Advantages

- Hot water instantly, 24 hours a day.
- Compact — takes up almost no space, ideal for condominium bathrooms.
- No reheating loss, unlike a storage tank.
- Relatively simple to replace.

### Limitations

- One shower point per unit.
- Output temperature depends on water pressure — low pressure gives weaker hot water.
- Needs a dedicated power circuit because of the high load.

If you need hot water in two bathrooms or the kitchen at once, a [storage tank heater](/services/water-heater/storage-tank-heater-installation) is a better fit.

## What Installation Includes

A correct instant heater installation covers more than mounting the unit:

1. **Wall check** — the wall must hold the unit securely (concrete or brick; for gypsum partitions, a reinforced backing is needed).
2. **Dedicated circuit** — the heater should have its own 20A point with an isolator, not share a circuit with other appliances.
3. **RCCB protection** — the circuit must include an earth-leakage device. If your home's DB box has no RCCB, we will flag this and recommend a fix — see our guide on [RCCB tripping](/problems/rccb-tripping-kl) for background.
4. **Anti-splash mounting and sealing** — the inlet and outlet are sealed so water cannot reach live parts; the unit is mounted at the correct height with the showerhead bracket fitted.
5. **Testing** — earth continuity, polarity, and the RCCB test button are verified before handover.

## Choosing an Instant Heater

Popular brands in Malaysia include Joven and Alpha for reliable budget-to-mid-range units, Panasonic and Rubine for solid mid-range options, and Midea for value. When choosing, check:

- **Power rating** — 3.5kW is standard; 4.5kW heats faster but needs a heavier circuit.
- **Safety certifications** — look for SIRIM or equivalent approval marking.
- **Warranty** — most brands carry a 1–2 year warranty; keep your receipt and warranty card.
- **Showerhead included?** — many units come with a shower set; check before you buy.

## Common Installation Mistakes

- **Using an existing socket on a shared circuit** — the heater trips breakers or damages wiring when other appliances run at the same time.
- **Mounting on a plasterboard wall** — without reinforcement, the unit can pull loose.
- **Skipping the RCCB** — a heater with an earth fault is a lethal shock risk without earth-leakage protection.
- **Poor sealing** — water seeping into the unit causes internal corrosion and short circuits.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to install an instant water heater in Malaysia?

### Do I need a new power point for an instant water heater?

Usually yes. An instant heater draws 3.5kW–4.5kW, so it needs a dedicated 20A point with an isolator. Reusing a general 13A socket is unsafe and will trip the breaker.

### Can an instant heater be installed in a condominium?

Yes. We install instant heaters in condominiums across KL and Selangor, coordinating with building management where required. Condominium bathrooms are one of the most common installation settings.

### How long does installation take?

A straightforward installation takes about one to two hours. If a new circuit needs to be run from the DB box, allow longer.

### Can I install a heater the electrician supplies, or bring my own?

Both. We can quote supply and installation together, or install a unit you have purchased — the same installation standard applies either way.
