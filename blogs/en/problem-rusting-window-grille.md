# Window Grille or Gate Rusting Quickly: Why and What to Do

**Question:** My window grille was only painted recently, but rust spots are already appearing, especially near the welds and joints. Why does it rust so fast, and can it be fixed?

**Answer:** Fast rusting is almost always a finishing problem, not a fault in the metal itself. Mild steel rusts when moisture and air reach it, and that happens when the surface was not properly prepared, a low-grade primer or paint was used, or the finish was scratched or chipped exposing bare metal. Malaysian humidity — and coastal air in particular — accelerates the process. The durable fix is to sand or blast back to clean metal, treat any remaining rust, and apply a proper anti-rust primer plus a hard-wearing topcoat, or hot-dip galvanise and powder-coat for the longest life.

## Why Grilles and Gates Rust Quickly

### 1. Poor Surface Preparation
If the metal was painted over mill scale, old rust or grease, the new finish cannot bond and rust begins beneath it almost immediately.

### 2. Low-Quality Primer or Paint
A thin coat of ordinary paint without an anti-rust primer offers little protection in a humid climate, and rust shows through within months.

### 3. Humid or Coastal Exposure
High humidity and salty air speed up corrosion dramatically. Properties near the coast rust noticeably faster than inland ones.

### 4. Scratches Exposing Bare Metal
Any chip or scratch that reaches the steel lets moisture in. Weld joints, hinges and screw holes are the usual first spots.

## How to Stop It for Good

| Approach | Best for | Durability |
|---|---|---|
| Sand + anti-rust primer + enamel | On-site touch-ups and repairs | Repaint every 3–5 years |
| Powder coating | Factory finish on new grilles | 10+ years, UV and weather resistant |
| Hot-dip galvanising | Outdoor structural items | Longest life, thick zinc coat |
| Galvanise + powder coat | Gates and grilles in harsh areas | Maximum durability |

For most landed-house grilles and gates, a powder-coated finish is the standard recommendation because it is baked on and resists weather far better than on-site enamel.

## What a Proper Repair Involves

1. **Strip or blast the surface** back to clean steel, removing rust, scale and old paint.
2. **Treat any remaining rust** with a rust converter so it cannot spread.
3. **Apply an anti-rust primer** before the topcoat.
4. **Finish with powder coating** (factory, most durable) or a weather-resistant enamel (on-site).
5. **Reinspect welds and joints**, which rust first, and seal them properly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you repaint existing grilles?
Yes. We blast or sand the surface, treat the rust, and apply a proper primer plus topcoat system so the finish lasts.

### Is powder coating better than paint?
Yes for outdoor metal. Powder coating is factory-applied and baked, giving far better UV and weather resistance than on-site enamel paint.

### Why does rust always start at the welds?
Welds and joints are where paint is thinnest and where moisture collects, so they are usually the first place rust appears if preparation was rushed.

### Do I need to remove the grille to treat it?
Many grilles can be treated on-site, but the most durable finishes — powder coating and galvanising — require the piece to be taken to a workshop.

### Is fast rust a safety concern?
Cosmetic surface rust is not usually structural, but if rust is flaking deeply or the metal is thinning at joints, it should be assessed before it weakens the frame.
