# RCCB Keeps Tripping: How to Find the Faulty Circuit Safely

**Question:** My main RCCB or ELCB keeps tripping, sometimes when I switch on the air-conditioner or water heater, sometimes for no obvious reason. What is causing it?

**Answer:** An RCCB trips because it has detected current leaking to earth — meaning current is flowing somewhere it should not, usually through damaged insulation, a wet connection, or a failing appliance element. It is a protective device doing its job. Common causes in Malaysian homes are a faulty appliance (water heater elements and older air-conditioners are frequent culprits), moisture ingress in an outdoor socket or the DB box, degraded wiring insulation, and accumulated small leakage across many circuits reaching the trip threshold. Repeatedly resetting an RCCB without diagnosis is unsafe.

**Never bypass, tape down, or replace an RCCB with a plain breaker to "solve" nuisance tripping.** The RCCB is the device that protects people from electric shock.

## What an RCCB Actually Does

An RCCB (Residual Current Circuit Breaker), often called an ELCB in Malaysia, continuously compares the current flowing out on the live conductor with the current returning on the neutral. In a healthy circuit these match. If some current is escaping to earth — through a person, through damp insulation, through a faulty appliance — the two no longer balance, and the RCCB disconnects the supply within milliseconds.

Domestic RCCBs are typically rated to trip at around 30 mA of residual current, a level chosen to protect against fatal electric shock.

This is important to understand for one reason: an RCCB tripping is not a nuisance to be worked around. It is evidence.

## The Safe Half-Split Test You Can Do Yourself

This narrows the fault down without any tools. Stop and call an electrician at any point where you find damage, water or a burning smell.

1. **Switch off every MCB (individual circuit breaker) in the DB box.**
2. **Reset the RCCB.** If it will not stay on with all circuits off, the fault is in the DB itself or the incoming wiring — stop here and call an electrician.
3. **Switch on one MCB at a time**, waiting a few seconds between each.
4. **Note which circuit trips it.** That circuit contains the fault.
5. **Within that circuit, unplug every appliance**, reset, and plug them back in one at a time. A single faulty appliance will trip it again when reconnected.
6. **If the circuit trips with nothing plugged in**, the fault is in the fixed wiring or an installed fitting — that is an electrician's job.

If the RCCB trips only intermittently, note what is running at the time. A pattern such as "only during heavy rain" or "only when the water heater runs" is genuinely useful diagnostic information.

## The Most Common Causes

| Cause | Typical clue |
|---|---|
| Faulty water heater element | Trips within seconds of switching the heater on |
| Older or damaged air-conditioner | Trips on compressor start-up |
| Water in an outdoor socket, garden light or DB box | Trips during or after rain |
| Degraded cable insulation in older wiring | Trips randomly, may worsen over time |
| Washing machine, fridge or kettle with a failing element | Trips when a specific appliance runs |
| Cumulative leakage across many circuits | Trips when several appliances run together |
| Rodent or nail damage to concealed cable | Trips after drilling, renovation or a long period undisturbed |

Cumulative leakage deserves a note: every appliance leaks a tiny amount of current to earth. Individually harmless, but a household with many appliances on one RCCB can sit just below the trip threshold — so one more appliance tips it over. The fix is splitting the installation across multiple RCCBs rather than protecting everything with one.

## Stop Immediately and Call If You See These

- Any burning smell, scorch mark or discolouration near the DB box or a socket.
- A socket, plug or switch plate that is warm to the touch.
- Buzzing or crackling from the DB box.
- Tripping that starts immediately after water entered the area.
- The RCCB will not reset at all with every circuit switched off.
- Any exposed or damaged cable.

These indicate an active fault with fire or shock risk, not a nuisance trip.

## What a Professional Diagnosis Involves

An ST-registered electrician will typically:

- Perform insulation resistance testing on each circuit to find degraded cable.
- Measure the actual leakage current per circuit to identify cumulative loading.
- Test the RCCB's own trip time and current — RCCBs do fail, and an old one can become over-sensitive.
- Inspect terminations in the DB box for loose or heat-damaged connections.
- Check earthing and bonding integrity.
- Recommend either a targeted repair, appliance replacement, or a DB reconfiguration that splits circuits across multiple RCCBs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it safe to keep resetting the RCCB?
No. Repeated tripping indicates a real earth fault. Resetting without diagnosis leaves a shock and fire risk in place.

### Can the RCCB itself be faulty?
Yes. RCCBs age and can become over-sensitive or fail to trip at all. Testing the device is part of a proper diagnosis, but a faulty RCCB should only be assumed after circuit faults are ruled out.

### Why does it only trip when it rains?
Water is reaching an outdoor socket, a garden light, a poorly sealed DB box, or damaged external cable insulation. This is a common and important pattern to report.

### Can you fix it without replacing the whole DB box?
Often, yes. Once the faulty circuit or appliance is isolated, the repair may be limited to that circuit. A DB reconfiguration is recommended when cumulative leakage or an outdated board is the underlying issue.

### How long does diagnosis take?
Usually 30–60 minutes for systematic circuit-by-circuit testing. Intermittent faults can take longer because the conditions have to be reproduced.

### Should I just replace the RCCB with a normal breaker?
No, under no circumstances. A standard MCB protects the cable against overload; it provides no protection against electric shock. Removing RCCB protection is dangerous.
