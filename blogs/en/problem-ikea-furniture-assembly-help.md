# Flat-Pack Furniture Assembly Help: When to Call Someone In

**Question:** I bought a flat-pack wardrobe or bed and cannot get it assembled properly. Is it worth paying someone, and what does the service actually include?

**Answer:** Assembly help is worth it when the item is tall or heavy, when it needs two people to lift or square up, when it must be anchored to the wall for safety, or when a previous attempt has left it unstable. A professional assembly visit typically covers sorting parts and hardware, assembling the frame square and level, securing tall or heavy units to the wall where required, adjusting doors, drawers and hinges, and removing packaging. Cost is based on item size, item count, complexity and whether wall drilling is required.

Flat-pack furniture is designed to be assembled at home, but the instructions assume ideal conditions: enough floor space, a second pair of hands, a level floor and complete hardware. Malaysian condominium bedrooms often provide none of those.

## When You Genuinely Need Help

| Situation | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Tall wardrobe or bookcase over about 1.5 m | Must be anchored to the wall; tip-over risk is real, especially with children |
| Item weighs more than one person can lift safely | Panels get racked or corners chipped when handled alone |
| Assembly must happen in a tight room | Many designs are built flat and then stood up — needs clearance |
| Wall anchoring into gypsum partition | Standard plugs will not hold; the correct anchor must be selected |
| Previous assembly is wobbly or misaligned | Usually a squaring error at the frame stage, which has to be corrected from the bottom up |
| Kitchen or bathroom units near water and power | Fixings and clearances need checking before installation |

## Why Anchoring Tall Furniture Is Not Optional

A tall chest of drawers or wardrobe becomes unstable as soon as an upper drawer is opened, and dramatically so if a child climbs on it. Furniture tip-over is a recognised child-safety hazard internationally, which is why manufacturers such as IKEA supply wall-anchoring hardware with tall items and instruct that it be used.

The complication in Malaysian homes is the wall itself. The supplied anchor is usually intended for solid masonry. On a gypsum partition or lightweight block wall, it must be substituted with an anchor rated for that construction — the same principle covered in [unsafe TV wall mounts](/problems/tv-fell-off-wall).

Anchor tall furniture if:
- It is taller than roughly 1.5 m, or top-heavy.
- Children or pets have access to the room.
- It sits on a soft floor covering that allows rocking.
- Drawers can be pulled out far enough to shift the centre of gravity.

## What a Professional Assembly Visit Covers

1. **Inventory and hardware sort.** Parts checked against the manual before starting. Missing hardware discovered mid-build is the most common cause of an abandoned assembly.
2. **Square frame assembly.** The frame is built square and checked with a level. A frame built out of square is why doors never align later.
3. **Floor levelling.** Feet or shims adjusted for uneven floors — common in older properties and after floor overlays.
4. **Wall anchoring where required.** Correct anchor selected for the actual wall type, with a check for concealed cables and pipes before drilling.
5. **Door, drawer and hinge adjustment.** Gaps evened out and soft-close mechanisms set so doors sit flush.
6. **Test and clean-up.** Every door and drawer cycled, and packaging cleared.

## What Cannot Always Be Fixed

- **Missing hardware.** Cam locks, dowels and specific screws are proprietary. If a part is missing, it has to be sourced from the manufacturer — an assembly visit cannot conjure it.
- **Damaged panels.** A chipped edge can be tidied; a cracked cam-lock housing or blown-out particleboard edge usually means a replacement panel.
- **Over-tightened or stripped fixings.** Particleboard has limited thread strength. Once a cam or screw hole is stripped, that fixing point is weakened permanently and may need a repair insert.
- **Repeatedly disassembled units.** Flat-pack furniture is designed for one assembly. Each disassembly weakens the joints; after two or three moves, some items are no longer worth rebuilding.

## If You Are Assembling It Yourself

- Read the whole manual before starting, not step by step as you go.
- Lay out and count every part first.
- Work on cardboard to protect both the floor and the panel faces.
- Do not fully tighten anything until the frame is confirmed square.
- Fit the back panel before standing the unit up — it is what holds the frame square.
- Anchor it to the wall before loading it.
- Keep the leftover hardware and the manual; they matter if you ever move.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do you assemble IKEA and Taobao furniture?
Yes — common flat-pack wardrobes, beds, desks, cabinets, shelves and storage units are all standard assembly work.

### Do you anchor wardrobes to the wall?
Yes, and it is recommended wherever safety requires it, particularly for tall units and in homes with children. The anchor is matched to the actual wall construction.

### Can you fix furniture that was assembled wrongly?
Often yes. Most problems trace back to a frame built out of square and can be corrected by partial disassembly. Damaged panels or missing proprietary hardware may need replacement parts.

### How is assembly priced?
By item size, number of items, complexity, and whether wall drilling is needed. A single bedside table and a six-door wardrobe are very different jobs.

### How long does a wardrobe take?
A small unit can be under an hour. A large multi-door wardrobe with drawers and anchoring commonly takes two to four hours.
