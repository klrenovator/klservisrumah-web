# Gypsum Board Partition Walls in KL and Selangor

**Question:** Can I add a room divider wall without hacking and brickwork?

**Answer:** Yes. A gypsum board partition is a lightweight framed wall — metal studs clad with gypsum board on both sides — that can divide a large room, create a study corner, section a shoplot or close off a storage area without wet brickwork. It goes up quickly, creates almost no mess compared to masonry, and arrives paint-ready once the joints are treated and skimmed. The trade-off: it is not a load-bearing wall, sound control depends on what goes inside the frame, and wet areas need moisture-resistant board or a different build altogether.

## Common Uses in KL & Selangor Properties

- **Extra bedroom or nursery** — dividing a large master bedroom or living area.
- **Home office / study corner** — a dedicated workspace with a door.
- **Storeroom** — closing off a corner of the utility or kitchen area.
- **Shoplot and office sectioning** — back-office, pantry or meeting-room partitions in commercial units.

## Gypsum Partition vs Brick Wall

| Factor | Gypsum board partition | Brick/block wall |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | Light — suits upper floors without extra loading checks | Heavy — adds structural load |
| Build speed | Fast, dry construction | Slow, wet trades, curing time |
| Mess | Minimal dust and no plaster curing mess | Hacking debris, mortar, wet plaster |
| Strength | Not for heavy cantilevered loads without reinforcement | Solid, mounts heavy items easily |
| Sound | Depends on stud spacing and insulation infill | Naturally better mass |
| Moisture | Standard board dislikes wet zones; MR board for damp | Tolerates wet areas well |

For most dry interior divisions in landed homes, condos and shops, gypsum partitions win on speed, weight and cost. Where a wall must take heavy wall-hung loads (kitchen wall cabinets, boilers, heavy shelves), the frame needs plywood reinforcement at those points, or brick is the better choice.

## What Goes Into a Proper Partition

1. **Layout and marking** — position the wall, check floors/ceilings are level, and confirm no concealed services are in the fixing line.
2. **Metal stud framing** — floor and ceiling tracks with vertical studs at regular centres, braced where the wall carries a door.
3. **Door frame** — a partition with a door needs a properly braced frame so daily slamming does not rack the wall.
4. **Boarding** — gypsum board screwed to both sides; moisture-resistant (MR) board in damp-adjacent spots; board joints staggered so seams do not line up.
5. **Optional infill** — rockwool or similar insulation inside the cavity for better sound control and a less hollow feel.
6. **Joint treatment** — tape and joint compound on every seam and screw head, sanded smooth.
7. **Finishing** — skim and sand where a fine finish is needed, then seal and paint like any other wall.

## Electrics and Mounting — Plan Before Boarding

Partitions go up fast, so decisions about them come early:

- **Power points and switches** for the new room must be wired inside the frame before the second face is boarded. That is an electrician's scope — see [lighting point & downlight installation](/services/electrical/lighting-point-and-downlight-installation) for added points.
- **Wall-mounted items** (TVs, shelves, cabinets) need plywood blocking inside the frame at the exact mounting zone, decided before closing the wall. A mounted TV on an unreinforced partition is exactly how [TVs fall off walls](/problems/tv-fell-off-wall).
- **Air-conditioning** — new enclosed rooms usually need their own cooling and ventilation plan; a partitioned room with no airflow path stays stuffy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a gypsum partition allowed in a condo unit?
Interior non-structural partitions are generally allowed, but condo managements typically require renovation approval before works start, and wet-area changes are a different matter. Confirm with your management office before scheduling.

### How strong is a gypsum partition wall?
It handles normal household contact, but it is not a masonry wall. Heavy wall-mounted loads need reinforcement built into the frame at those points, and exposed corners in high-traffic areas benefit from corner protection.

### Can it be removed later if I change the layout?
Yes — that is one of its biggest advantages. Dismantling a gypsum partition is far simpler than hacking out a brick wall, making it a sensible choice for rental units and evolving layouts.
