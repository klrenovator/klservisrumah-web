A statement light over a dining table, kitchen island, or entryway instantly changes a room. But pendants and chandeliers are heavy, and the ceiling mounting must carry both the weight and the strain of the fixture. This guide explains what a safe installation includes and what to consider before buying.

## What Counts as a Pendant or Chandelier?

- **Pendants** — a single light suspended from the ceiling on a cord or rod. Available as one fixture or in clusters of three, five, or more.
- **Chandeliers** — multi-arm decorative fixtures, ranging from lightweight crystal styles to heavy brass or iron designs.
- **Linear lights** — long horizontal fixtures, popular over kitchen islands and dining tables.
- **Globe/bubble lights** — rounded glass designs that diffuse light softly.

## Installation: What's Included

1. **Weight assessment** — the installer checks the fixture's weight and the ceiling type (concrete, plaster, or gypsum false ceiling).
2. **Reinforced hook** — for anything heavier than a lightweight shade, a reinforced ceiling hook is installed and anchored to the structure above. For gypsum false ceilings (common in condominiums), a steel support frame is fixed to the concrete slab, then the hook attaches to it.
3. **Safety cable** — heavy fixtures are fitted with a secondary safety cable, so even if the primary hook ever failed, the fixture would not fall.
4. **Wiring** — the fixture is wired to the ceiling point with correct polarity and a secure connection, concealed inside the canopy.
5. **Level & alignment** — the fixture is hung level and aligned over the table or island, at the right height.
6. **Testing** — all bulbs and circuits are tested before handover.

Installation carries a 12-month lighting installation warranty.

## How High Should a Pendant Hang?

- **Dining table** — the bottom of the fixture should hang about 70–80 cm above the tabletop.
- **Kitchen island** — 80–90 cm above the counter.
- **Entryway** — high enough to clear the tallest person; fixtures with chains can be adjusted.
- **Bedside** — just above eye level when sitting.

These are starting points — the installer will position the fixture to suit your ceiling height and room layout.

## Choosing the Right Fixture

- **Size** — a common guide: add the room's length and width in feet, and that number (in inches) is a reasonable fixture diameter. For example, a 12 × 10 ft dining room suits a fixture around 22 inches wide.
- **Weight** — check the manufacturer's weight; anything above a few kilograms needs the reinforced mounting described above.
- **Bulb type and brightness** — LED bulbs are the sensible choice in Malaysia; confirm the fixture accepts them and the wattage suits the space.
- **Ceiling height** — low ceilings suit compact pendants or flush-mount options; high ceilings allow dramatic multi-tier fixtures.

### Can a chandelier be installed on a gypsum false ceiling?

Yes, but never directly into the gypsum board. The fixture must be supported by a steel frame anchored to the concrete slab above, with a safety cable. This is standard practice for condominium installations.

### What is a safety cable for?

A safety cable is a secondary steel wire connecting the fixture to the structure. If the primary hook ever failed, the cable would catch the fixture and prevent it from falling — an important safeguard for heavy or expensive lights.
