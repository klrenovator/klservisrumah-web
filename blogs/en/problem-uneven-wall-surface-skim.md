# Uneven or Bumpy Wall Surface: When Paint Is Not Enough

**Question:** My walls look bumpy under sidelight. Old patches and plaster ridges show through the new paint. Do I need a full skim, or just more paint?

**Answer:** Paint does not hide bumps. Filler is for spot repairs. A skim coat is a 2–3 mm full-wall thin plaster layer that gives a flat, premium finish. Very uneven walls may need more than one coat. After curing, the wall is sanded, sealed and then painted.

This is an **uneven surface** problem. If the skim is peeling off, see [peeling skim coat](/problems/peeling-skim-coat). If the film is cracking in hairlines, see [paint cracking hairline walls](/problems/paint-cracking-hairline-walls).

## Why Walls Look Bumpy

- Old textured paint or plaster ridges.
- Uneven original wall.
- Multiple paint layers building up unevenly.
- Repair patches that were never smoothed.

## Skim Coat vs Filler

| Job | Use |
|---|---|
| One hole or crack | Filler |
| Whole wall looks wavy under sidelight | Full-wall skim |
| Premium flat modern finish | Skim, then paint |
| Old rental refresh | Often a full-room skim |

## What the Work Involves

1. Sand rough areas.
2. Apply a 2–3 mm skim (more layers if the wall is very uneven).
3. Sand after curing.
4. Prime with sealer before painting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is skim coat different from filler?
Yes. Filler is for spots. Skim coat is a full-wall thin plaster layer.

### How thick is a skim coat?
Standard is 2 to 3 mm. Very uneven walls may need multiple layers.

### Can I skip skim if I am repainting?
You can, but paint alone does not hide bumps. Premium finishes start with a proper skim.
