# Digital and Smart Lock Installation on an Existing Door

**Question:** Can a digital or smart lock be installed on an existing door?

**Answer:** Most timber, laminate and metal doors can accept a digital lock if the door thickness, backset, lock body and strike position match the model. The main risks are buying the wrong lock body, forcing a mortice that weakens the door, or misaligning the strike so the lock binds. A locksmith can verify fit before purchase and install it without damaging the door.

This is an **installation article**, separate from [smart lock not working](/problems/smart-lock-not-working), which covers faults such as dead batteries, jammed bolts and fingerprint failure.

## Check Before You Buy

| Factor | Why it matters | Typical requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Door thickness | Determines spindle and bolt length | Often 35–60 mm, depending on brand |
| Backset | Distance from door edge to handle centre | Usually 60 mm or 70 mm |
| Lock body size | Must fit the existing mortice or suit a new cut-out | Check dimensions carefully |
| Door material | Timber, laminate and metal gates need different fittings | Use correct screws and drill bits |
| Left/right opening | Handles and latch direction must match | May be reversible |
| Strike alignment | Deadbolt must enter the hole freely | Adjust strike plate |

## Installation Steps

### 1. Confirm compatibility
Measure door thickness, backset and the existing mortice. For metal gates, verify whether a gate-specific digital lock is needed rather than a residential door lock.

### 2. Remove the old lock
The old handle, cylinder and mortice body are removed carefully. Existing holes can often be reused, but some digital locks require a larger opening.

### 3. Fit the new mortice and bolt
The lock body must sit square in the door edge. A crooked mortice causes binding and drains batteries faster.

### 4. Install interior and exterior units
Cables must not be pinched. Spindle, tail bar and mounting posts should fit without force.

### 5. Align the strike and test
Test the fingerprint, PIN, RFID card, mechanical key and app features. Confirm that the deadbolt extends smoothly whether the door is open and closed.

## Common Installation Mistakes

- **Buying the wrong backset:** the latch does not line up with the old hole.
- **Over-tightening screws:** can pinch the cable or bend the mounting plate.
- **Ignoring door warp:** a door that already touches the frame may jam after the deadbolt is added.
- **Forgetting the mechanical key:** digital locks still need a backup key; store it securely outside the house.
- **Installing on a weak door:** a strong lock on a thin or damaged door gives false security.

## Door Types and Considerations

### Timber and laminate main doors
Most suitable, but check whether existing handle holes will be covered by the new escutcheon plate.

### Metal gates
Gate locks are exposed to rain and need weatherproofing, a different strike and often a heavier-duty latch.

### Condo doors
Some management committees restrict exterior lock changes. Check your by-laws before modifying a common-property fire door. This article does not claim any digital lock is a certified fire-rated lock.

## Maintenance After Installation

- Replace batteries before they leak; most locks give low-battery warnings.
- Keep the mechanical key outside the home.
- Clean the fingerprint sensor gently.
- Do not oil electronic keypads; use a dry cloth.
- Check the strike plate if the door starts to bind.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can any smart lock fit my existing door?
Not every lock fits every door. Door thickness, backset, mortice size, door material and opening direction must match the lock specification.

### Do I need to change the whole door?
Usually not. If the door is solid and existing holes can be adapted, a locksmith can fit the digital lock without replacing it.

### What if the smart lock runs out of battery?
Most models have a low-battery warning, external power contacts or a mechanical key override. Keep the key outside the house.
