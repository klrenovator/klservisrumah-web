# Built-in Wardrobe Door Not Closing or Misaligned

**Question:** One of my built-in wardrobe doors does not close flush — it sits proud of the other door, rubs against the frame, or swings back open. Is the wardrobe faulty?

**Answer:** A wardrobe door that will not close is usually an alignment problem, not a structural failure. The three main causes are a frame that was not perfectly level when installed, worn or drifted hinges, and a door panel that has warped in Malaysian humidity. Each has a different fix: levelling and re-securing the frame, adjusting or replacing the hinges, or planning and re-hanging the door. In most cases the wardrobe can be corrected in place at a fraction of the cost of a new build.

## Why Wardrobe Doors Stop Closing

### 1. Frame Not Level
A built-in wardrobe is fitted between floor and ceiling, and even a few millimetres of run-out makes one door sit proud of the other. This is the most common cause in older installs.

### 2. Worn Hinges
Doors are opened many times a day, and hinge screws and springs drift and wear. A door that used to close and now hangs slightly low is nearly always hinge-related.

### 3. Warped Panels
Timber and board panels absorb and release moisture with the weather. A tall, thin door in a humid room can bow slightly and refuse to sit flush.

### 4. Overloaded Shelving
Heavy items on the wardrobe floor or a sagging shelf can twist the carcass just enough to throw the doors out of line.

## Reading the Symptoms

| Symptom | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| Door hangs lower on the hinge side | Worn or loose hinges |
| Doors sit proud of each other | Frame not level, or hinges drifted |
| Door rubs at top or bottom corner | Panel warped, or frame twisted |
| Door closes but pops back open | Hinge or catch misalignment |
| Gaps uneven all round | Carcass shifted or out of square |

## What a Proper Repair Involves

1. **Check the frame** for level and re-secure it if it has moved.
2. **Tighten and adjust hinges** first — this resolves most cases.
3. **Replace worn hinges** or fit soft-close hardware where wanted.
4. **Plane the door** only if it is genuinely warped, taking off the minimum material.
5. **Re-hang and re-square** so gaps are even.

For doors that are heavily warped or split, replacing the single panel is the better answer than repeatedly shaving a door that will keep moving.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you fix existing wardrobes?
Yes. We repair and adjust built-in wardrobes, replace hinges, realign doors and plane warped panels — no new build required in most cases.

### Do you use soft-close hinges?
Yes. We fit quality soft-close systems so doors close gently and stay shut.

### Why does my wardrobe door only misbehave in humid weather?
Timber and board expand in humidity. A door that is borderline in dry weather can bow or bind once the air becomes damp.

### Is a warped door a lost cause?
Not usually. Minor warp can be corrected by planing and re-hanging; only a badly split or heavily warped panel needs replacing.

### How long does a wardrobe door repair take?
A single-door hinge adjustment is usually under an hour. Levelling a frame or planing a door typically takes a visit or two including finishing.
