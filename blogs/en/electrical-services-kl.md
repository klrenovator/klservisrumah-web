## What Counts as Licensed Electrical Work in Malaysia

Under Malaysian regulations, most fixed electrical installation work — adding sockets, running new circuits, upgrading your DB box, or rewiring — must be carried out by a registered wireman. This includes the installation of new points, alteration of existing circuits, and work on your consumer unit.

Why this matters:

- **Safety** — a licensed wireman follows the national wiring regulations and tests insulation, earth continuity, and RCCB function before handing the job back.
- **Insurance** — if faulty DIY wiring causes a fire, insurance claims can be disputed or rejected.
- **Compliance** — strata management in condominiums across Mont Kiara, Bangsar, and other KL high-rises usually requires proof that electrical work was done by a registered contractor.
- **Accountability** — licensed work comes with a workmanship warranty, so you are covered if something fails later.

At KL Servis Rumah, all electrical jobs are ST-compliant: we plan the load, isolate power safely, install to code, and provide a written test record when the work is complete.

## Signs Your Home's Electrical System Needs Attention

Some warning signs should not be ignored:

- **Frequent tripping** — if your RCCB or MCB trips when you switch on a normal appliance, the circuit may be overloaded or leaking. See our guide on [RCCB tripping in KL homes](/problems/rccb-tripping-kl).
- **Flickering or dimming lights** — this can indicate loose connections or an overloaded circuit, not just a bad bulb. Read about [flickering downlights](/problems/flickering-downlights).
- **Warm switch plates or sockets** — heat behind a switch is a serious warning sign of loose or undersized wiring.
- **Old fuse boxes** — fuse wire and rewireable fuses offer no protection against earth leakage; a modern DB box with RCCB is the standard fix.
- **Two-pin sockets without earthing** — common in older terrace houses in Petaling Jaya, Klang, and Cheras. Grounding protects you and your appliances.
- **Burning smell** — if you smell burnt plastic near a socket, isolate the circuit and call an electrician immediately.

## Safety Tips for Homeowners

- Never run multiple high-power appliances from a single socket using adapters — this is a common cause of overheating in Malaysian homes.
- Test your RCCB monthly by pressing the test button; it should trip immediately.
- Keep water away from electrical outlets, especially in bathrooms and kitchens.
- If a socket feels warm, stop using it and have it inspected.
- Use a registered electrician for any fixed wiring work — extension sockets and adapters are not a permanent solution.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I really need a licensed electrician for one extra socket?

Yes. Adding a new socket means extending a circuit, which is fixed electrical installation work under Malaysian regulations. It must be done by a registered wireman — partly for safety, and partly so your insurance and building management accept the work.

### How much does it cost to add a power point in Malaysia?

A new 13A power point in KL or Selangor typically starts from RM 150 per point, depending on cable run and wall type. Multiple points done in one visit cost less per point because the cable and labour are shared.

### What is a DB box and why would I need to upgrade it?

The DB (distribution board) is the box that takes power from your meter and distributes it to the circuits in your home. Older boards with fuse wire provide no earth-leakage protection. Upgrading to an MCB + RCCB board protects people from electric shock and appliances from damage.

### How long does a DB box upgrade take?

A straightforward DB box upgrade is usually completed within a day. Full or partial rewiring takes longer and depends on the size of the property — your electrician will give you a realistic timeline with the quote.
