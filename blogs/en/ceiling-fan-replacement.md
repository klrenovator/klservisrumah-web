Fans wear out: motors burn, bearings grind, capacitors fail, and styles age. Replacing a 15-year-old fan with a modern DC model can also cut noise and energy use. This guide explains when replacement makes sense, what the job includes, and how to get a clean, safe result.

## Is It Better to Repair or Replace Your Ceiling Fan?

**Repair is worth it when:**
- The problem is simple — a worn capacitor, loose blade screws, or a faulty regulator.
- The fan is under 10 years old and parts are available.

**Replacement is the better choice when:**
- The motor is burnt out or the winding is damaged.
- The fan wobbles badly and the mounts are worn — read our guide on [wobbly ceiling fans](/problems/ceiling-fan-wobbling).
- The fan is old, noisy, or lacks the speed control you want.
- You want to upgrade to a DC fan with a remote or app.
- The existing fan has been exposed to water or has visible rust on motor parts.

## What Replacement Includes

1. **Power isolation** — the fan circuit is isolated and verified safe before any work.
2. **Removal** — the old fan is taken down carefully, including blades, canopy, and motor. The old hook is inspected.
3. **Hook check** — the existing hook is reused only if it is solidly anchored. If it is undersized, corroded, or loose, a heavy-duty hook is installed — this is critical for safety.
4. **New fan installation** — the new fan is mounted level, wired correctly (regulator for AC, remote/app pairing for DC), and all fittings tightened.
5. **Balancing and swing test** — blades are balanced and the fan is run at full speed to confirm smooth, quiet operation.

Disposal of the old fan is included, so you do not have to find a way to get rid of the bulky unit.

## Choosing a Replacement Fan

- **Match size to room** — measure the room and pick 42–48 inch (bedrooms) or 52–56 inch (living rooms). Larger spaces may need 60-inch or multiple fans.
- **AC vs DC** — DC fans are quieter and more efficient with more speed steps; AC fans are simpler and cheaper.
- **Light kit** — if the old fan included lighting, decide whether the new one should too.
- **Ceiling height** — low ceilings need flush mounts; high ceilings benefit from a downrod.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I keep the existing fan hook?

Usually, yes — if it is solidly anchored and correctly sized for the new fan. The installer inspects it before reuse. If there is any doubt, a new heavy-duty hook is installed for a small additional charge.

### Do you take away the old fan?

Yes, safe disposal of the old fan is part of the replacement service.

### Will a DC fan work with my existing wall regulator?

No — DC fans come with their own remote control and do not use a wall regulator. The installer adjusts the wiring accordingly.

### How long does a replacement take?

Typically one to one and a half hours for a standard ceiling. Jobs requiring a new hook, new wiring, or false-ceiling reinforcement take longer.

### Can the new fan be installed on the same wiring?

In most cases yes, the existing fan circuit is reused. If the wiring is damaged or the switch needs changing for a DC fan, we quote the additional work before starting.
