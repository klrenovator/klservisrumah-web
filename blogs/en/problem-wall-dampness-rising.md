# Rising Damp: Why the Bottom of Your Wall Keeps Peeling

**Question:** Why does the paint keep bubbling and flaking only along the bottom half metre of my wall, with a white powdery deposit, no matter how many times I repaint?

**Answer:** That pattern is rising damp. Moisture from the ground or from a wet area behind the wall is drawn upward through porous brickwork and plaster by capillary action. It carries dissolved salts with it. As the water evaporates at the wall surface, the salts crystallise — that is the white powder, called efflorescence — and the crystal growth pushes the paint film off the wall. Repainting fails because the moisture and the salt-contaminated plaster are still there. The repair sequence is: identify and cut the moisture source, remove contaminated plaster, apply a damp-control system, then replaster and repaint with a compatible coating.

Rising damp is common in older landed homes in Kuala Lumpur and Selangor, in ground-floor units, and on walls that back onto bathrooms, planters, drains or paved areas.

## The Signature: How to Recognise Rising Damp

| Sign | What it indicates |
|---|---|
| Damage confined to the lower 300–1,000 mm of the wall, with a fairly level top edge | Classic rising damp — water rises to the height where evaporation balances the supply |
| White, powdery or crystalline deposit | Efflorescence — salts left behind as moisture evaporates |
| Paint bubbling and lifting in sheets rather than flaking randomly | Salt crystallisation pushing the film off |
| Plaster feels soft, sandy or hollow when tapped | Salt-contaminated, degraded plaster |
| Musty smell near the skirting | Persistent moisture in the wall base |
| Timber skirting swollen or rotting | Long-term moisture at floor level |

If the damage is high on the wall, in patches, or spreading downward from a ceiling, it is not rising damp — see [damp walls and paint bubbling](/problems/damp-walls-paint-bubbling) for leak-driven dampness.

## The Four Real Sources

### 1. Ground Moisture Through Masonry
Brick, block and cement render are porous. Where a damp-proof course is absent, damaged, or bridged, groundwater wicks upward into the wall. This is the textbook mechanism and is most common in older landed properties.

### 2. External Ground Level or Paving Bridging the DPC
Concrete driveways, patios, planter beds and added paving are frequently laid higher than the original ground level, so rainwater sits against the wall above the damp-proof course. Water then enters the masonry directly above the barrier. This is one of the most common and most fixable causes.

### 3. A Leak Behind the Wall
A slow leak from a concealed pipe, a bathroom on the other side, or a shower wall with failed waterproofing produces damp at the base of the wall that looks identical to rising damp. It must be ruled out first — the repair is completely different.

### 4. Poor Drainage Against the Wall
Blocked perimeter drains, a downpipe discharging against the wall base, or ground that slopes toward the house keep the masonry saturated. In Malaysia's rainfall, this alone can sustain a permanent damp band.

## Why Waterproof Paint Alone Always Fails

Homeowners often try a "damp-proof" or waterproof coating over the affected area. It fails for two reasons:

1. **The salts are still in the plaster.** Hygroscopic salts absorb humidity from the air and keep the plaster damp even after the original source is removed. Crystallisation continues under the new coating.
2. **Sealing one face redirects the moisture.** A film-forming coating stops evaporation at that point. The water then rises higher or moves sideways, and the damage reappears above or beside the treated band, often worse than before.

Salt-contaminated plaster must be removed, not coated over.

## The Correct Repair Sequence

1. **Diagnose the source.** Establish whether the moisture is ground-borne, from external ground level, or from a leak behind the wall. Moisture readings taken at different heights indicate the pattern; a plumbing check rules out concealed pipework.
2. **Cut the source.** Lower external paving or form a drainage channel away from the wall, redirect downpipes, clear perimeter drains, or repair the leak. Skipping this step guarantees recurrence.
3. **Remove contaminated plaster.** Hack back the affected render, typically to around 300–500 mm above the highest visible damp line, back to sound masonry.
4. **Allow the wall to dry.** Drying takes time — commonly several weeks depending on wall thickness and weather. This is the step most often rushed.
5. **Apply a damp-control system.** A salt-retardant render or a suitable damp-proof system appropriate to the wall construction and diagnosis.
6. **Replaster and finish.** Skim to a smooth finish, then an alkali-resistant sealer and a breathable, washable topcoat. Do not use an impermeable coating on a wall that still needs to breathe.

## What Homeowners Get Wrong

- **Repainting immediately.** The most common and most expensive mistake — the new paint fails within months.
- **Sanding off the efflorescence and sealing.** Removing the visible salt does nothing about the salt still in the plaster.
- **Assuming all base-of-wall damp is rising damp.** A concealed pipe leak is the frequent alternative and needs [plumbing leak diagnosis](/services/plumbing/water-leakage-diagnosis-and-repair), not waterproofing.
- **Finishing before the wall is dry.** Trapped moisture pushes the new finish off just as the original one failed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What exactly is rising damp?
Moisture moving upward through porous wall materials by capillary action, from the ground or from an adjoining wet area, carrying dissolved salts that crystallise at the surface.

### Does rising damp happen in Malaysia?
Base-of-wall dampness is common here, driven by high rainfall, high ground moisture, paving laid above the damp-proof course, and poor perimeter drainage. Whether a given case is true ground-borne rising damp or a leak has to be confirmed on site.

### Can I fix rising damp with waterproof paint?
No. Coating over salt-contaminated plaster traps the moisture and pushes the damage higher up the wall.

### How long before I can repaint?
The wall must be dry after the source is cut and the plaster removed. That is commonly several weeks; repainting earlier is why so many repairs fail twice.

### Is rising damp a structural problem?
It is primarily a durability and finish problem, but sustained damp degrades plaster, rots timber skirting and door frames, and encourages mould, so it is worth addressing.

### How do I tell it apart from a leak?
Rising damp has a fairly level top edge and is confined to the wall base. A leak produces a patch that spreads from a point, is often higher up, and may worsen when a specific fixture is used.
