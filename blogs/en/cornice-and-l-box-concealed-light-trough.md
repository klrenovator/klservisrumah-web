# Cornice & L-Box Concealed Light Troughs in KL and Selangor

**Question:** What is the difference between a cornice and an L-box light trough?

**Answer:** A **cornice** is a decorative moulding that covers the joint where the wall meets the ceiling, giving the room a finished edge and hiding hairline cracks at that junction. An **L-box** is an L-shaped recessed section built around the perimeter of a plaster ceiling, designed to hide LED strip lighting so the ceiling glows with soft indirect light instead of showing visible fittings. Both are plaster or gypsum-based work done at the same stage of a ceiling project, and they are usually planned, framed and skimmed together.

## What a Cornice Does

- Covers the wall-ceiling junction with a decorative profile.
- Hides the hairline crack that forms where materials meet and move differently.
- Adds a classical or transitional finish to living rooms, bedrooms and foyers.
- Gives a visual "cap" that makes ceiling lines look straighter than bare plastered corners.

Cornices come in pre-formed profiles (plaster, gypsum or polyurethane) or are formed in-situ. The profile size should match the ceiling height — oversized cornices in a 9-foot room look heavy; a slim clean line suits modern interiors.

## What an L-Box Light Trough Does

An L-box is a dropped perimeter section of plaster ceiling with an open channel facing the room. Inside that channel sit LED strip lights:

- The light washes across the ceiling for ambient, glare-free illumination.
- No visible light fittings — the room appears lit by the architecture itself.
- It visually raises the ceiling by emphasising the perimeter.
- It works with tiered ceiling designs to create depth.

Because LED strips, drivers and switching must be coordinated with the electrics, the lighting plan should be finalised before the ceiling frame is closed. LED strip supply and install is a lighting scope — see [cove & strip LED installation](/services/lighting/cove-and-strip-led-installation).

## Where They Are Commonly Installed

- **Living room perimeter** — ambient light without downlight glare.
- **Master bedroom** — soft lighting behind or above the bed zone.
- **Foyer and hallway** — a continuous light line guiding the eye.
- **Dining area** — defines the dining zone in open-plan layouts.
- **TV/feature walls** — combined with coves for a layered effect.

## How They Are Built

1. **Design and set-out** — profile selection, trough width and drop height are marked on site with your ceiling plan.
2. **Framing** — light-gauge framing fixed to the slab forms the box or cornice line.
3. **Boarding** — the frame is clad with gypsum/plaster board; trough openings are formed cleanly.
4. **Jointing and skimming** — all joints and surfaces are skimmed smooth; corners are sharp and continuous.
5. **LED installation** — strips and drivers are set into the trough, wired to the planned switch or dimmer (electrical scope).
6. **Painting** — sealer and topcoats complete the finish; the trough interior is painted matte white for best light reflection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can an L-box be added to my existing ceiling?
Sometimes. If the existing ceiling is sound and there is headroom to frame the trough, a retrofit is possible. Where ceilings are low or the existing board is failing, building the L-box as part of a new ceiling is the more durable option.

### Do the LED strips come with the plaster work?
The trough, framing and finishing are the ceiling scope; the supply and installation of LED strips, drivers and switches is a lighting scope — see [cove & strip LED installation](/services/lighting/cove-and-strip-led-installation). Both are coordinated so the trough is sized for the strips and drivers have accessible placement.

### Why is my cornice cracking at the corners?
Seasonal movement at the wall-ceiling junction opens up corner mitres first. Stable cracks can be refilled and refinished; recurrent cracks may need the joint rebuilt with flexible treatment — see the [ceiling cornice crack](/problems/ceiling-cornice-crack) guide for when to repair vs replace.
