# Burst Pipe Emergency in KL & Selangor: Step-by-Step Guide

Question: What should I do immediately when a water pipe bursts in my house?

Answer: First, immediately shut off your home's main stopcock valve (usually located next to your outdoor Syabas/Air Selangor water meter or inside the kitchen wet area). Second, turn off your electrical distribution board (DB) main switch if water is pooling near outlets. Third, turn on all ground-level taps to drain residual water, and contact an emergency plumber with photos or video of the rupture.

A burst pipe in Kuala Lumpur or Selangor can discharge hundreds of litres of water per minute, resulting in ruined timber flooring, damaged electrical circuits, collapsed plaster ceilings, and massive water bills. Fast, decisive containment action is critical.

## 4 Immediate Steps to Take in the First 5 Minutes

1. **Shut Off the Main Water Stopcock:**
   - In landed houses: Locate the brass lever or wheel valve on the incoming pipe beside the water meter near your front gate. Turn it clockwise until tight.
   - In condominiums: Check the utility riser cabinet in the common corridor or under the kitchen sink.
2. **Isolate Electrical Power:**
   - If water is pooling near skirting sockets or dripping through light fixtures, shut off the main circuit breaker at your DB box to eliminate electrocution hazards.
3. **Relieve Internal Pipe Pressure:**
   - Open all outdoor garden taps and lowest bathroom faucets to drain residual pressurized water out of the system away from living areas.
4. **Document & Clear Excess Water:**
   - Capture quick photos/videos for insurance claims and message your plumber, then use mops and buckets to protect [laminate or parquet flooring](/services/flooring).

## Why Do Pipes Burst in Malaysian Homes?

| Cause of Burst | How It Happens | Most Vulnerable Pipe Material |
|---|---|---|
| **High Municipal Pressure Surges** | Sudden water pressure spikes at night when neighbourhood water usage drops | PVC / ABS pipes with weak solvent joints |
| **Aged Galvanised Iron (GI) Corrosion** | Internal rust eats through pipe wall thickness, causing blowout under normal pressure | Old galvanized iron pipes (in houses >15 years old) |
| **Failed Solvent Cement Joints** | Degraded PVC glue or poor insertion depth during renovation plumbing | Standard uPVC cold water pipes |
| **Accidental Drilling Penetration** | Drilling into walls to mount TV brackets, mirrors, or shelving | Concealed PPR or copper pipes |
