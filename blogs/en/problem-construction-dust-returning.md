# Fine Construction Dust Returning After a Post-Reno Clean

**Question:** I had my unit cleaned after renovation, but a fine white or grey dust keeps settling on every surface days later. Is the cleaning job incomplete?

**Answer:** Not necessarily — and it is a common outcome when the clean did not reach where renovation dust hides. Fine dust from sanding, plastering and tiling does not just sit on surfaces; it drifts into the false ceiling, the air-conditioning and ventilation ducts, gaps behind cabinetry, and porous materials. Days later, normal air movement stirs it back out and it settles again. The fix is a clean that targets these hidden reservoirs — HEPA vacuuming, cleaning inside the false ceiling and ducts, wiping all surfaces with microfiber, and running an air scrubber to capture what is still floating.

## Where Renovation Dust Hides

| Hiding place | Why it keeps coming back |
|---|---|
| False ceiling void | Dust settles above the boards and drifts down through gaps |
| Air-conditioning ducts and filters | The system recirculates dust every time it runs |
| Behind and on top of cabinetry | Sheltered areas missed by a surface clean |
| Porous surfaces (grout, raw wood) | Dust lodges in pores and releases slowly |
| Window tracks and light fittings | Ledge surfaces that catch and shed dust |

## Why a Standard Clean Is Not Enough

A normal post-renovation clean removes dust from visible surfaces. But renovation dust is extremely fine and remains airborne for a long time, so unless the hidden reservoirs and the air itself are addressed, the dust simply resettles. The two things that make the difference are:

- **HEPA filtration** on vacuums, which traps fine dust instead of blowing it back out.
- **Air scrubbing**, which pulls floating dust out of the air while the cleaning is in progress.

## What a Proper Post-Reno Clean Involves

1. **HEPA-vacuum every surface**, including tops of cabinets, ledges and window tracks.
2. **Clean inside the false ceiling and ducts** where accessible, and change or clean filters.
3. **Wipe all surfaces with microfiber** — dry cloths just push fine dust around.
4. **Clean grout and porous surfaces** so dust does not keep releasing.
5. **Run an air scrubber** to capture airborne dust before final detailing.
6. **Final pass** once the air has settled, so the unit is genuinely move-in ready.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does dust keep coming back after cleaning?
Fine renovation dust settles in hidden areas — the false ceiling, ducts and porous surfaces — and is disturbed back out by air movement.

### Do you use special equipment?
Yes. We use HEPA vacuums, air scrubbers and negative air machines for post-renovation cleans, which trap fine dust rather than recirculate it.

### Is returning dust harmful?
Fine construction dust can irritate the lungs and aggravate allergies or respiratory conditions, so it is worth removing thoroughly before moving in.

### How is this different from regular cleaning?
Post-renovation cleaning targets construction residue — cement, grout haze, silicone and fine dust — and includes HEPA vacuuming and air scrubbing that routine cleaning does not.

### How long does a full post-renovation clean take?
A standard 1,000 sqft condo typically takes a day with a small team; larger units or heavier renovation take longer.
