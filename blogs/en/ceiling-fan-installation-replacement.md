A ceiling fan is only as safe as its mounting. The most critical step is the fan hook: it must be solidly anchored to the structure above, because a fan that comes loose while spinning is a serious hazard. ## Types of Ceiling Fans We Install

### AC motor fans

The traditional workhorse — affordable, reliable, and available in a wide range of styles and sizes.

- Standard choice for most bedrooms, living rooms, and halls.
- Simple operation with pull chains or wall regulators.
- Popular brands: KDK, Panasonic, Fanco, Khind.

### DC motor (smart) fans

Modern fans with efficient DC motors, remote controls, and app pairing.

- Quieter, more energy-efficient, and often reversible for year-round use.
- More precise speed control (6–12 speeds) and a sleep timer.
- Good for bedrooms and living areas where noise matters.
- Slightly higher price, but lower running cost.

### Industrial / commercial fans

Large 60-inch and high-CFM fans for warehouses, gyms, shops, and covered outdoor areas.

- Heavy-duty mounting and wiring.
- Designed for air movement over large open areas.

## What a Safe Installation Includes

A proper ceiling fan installation involves more than hanging the fan:

1. **Hook inspection or replacement** — the existing fan hook is checked; if it is missing, undersized, or not anchored to a solid beam, a heavy-duty hook is installed.
2. **Wiring** — the fan circuit is connected properly, with concealed or trunked wiring, a correct regulator (for AC fans) or receiver (for DC fans), and secure terminations.
3. **Mounting** — the fan is mounted level on the hook or ceiling plate, with all screws torqued and the canopy closed cleanly.
4. **Blade balancing** — blades are fitted and balanced so the fan runs without wobbling.
5. **Swing test** — the fan is run at full speed and checked for vibration, noise, and wobble before handover.

Installation carries a 12-month mounting and wiring warranty.

## Choosing the Right Fan for Your Room

| Room size | Recommended fan size |
|---|---|
| Small bedroom / study (up to ~120 sq ft) | 42–48 inch |
| Master bedroom / living room (up to ~250 sq ft) | 52–56 inch |
| Large hall / open plan / commercial space | 56–60+ inch, multiple fans or high-CFM units |

Also consider ceiling height: on low ceilings, a flush or low-profile mount works better; on high ceilings, a downrod lowers the fan to where the airflow matters.

## When to Replace Instead of Repair

A fan wobble, noise, or slow speed is often repairable — loose blades can be rebalanced and worn capacitors replaced. Replacement makes more sense when:

- The motor is worn or burnt out.
- The fan is more than 10–15 years old and parts are hard to find.
- You want a quieter DC fan or a different style for the room.
- The fan has been through damage or water exposure.

If your current fan wobbles or swings, our guide on [wobbly ceiling fans](/problems/ceiling-fan-wobbling) explains the causes and fixes.

### Can a ceiling fan be installed on a gypsum ceiling?

Yes, but only with a reinforced mount — a fan cannot be hung directly from plasterboard. In condominiums with gypsum ceilings, we install a heavy-duty support (such as a steel angle or specialist fan support) anchored to the concrete slab above.

### How long does installation take?

A straightforward installation takes about one hour. Jobs that need a new hook, new wiring, or work on a false ceiling take longer.
