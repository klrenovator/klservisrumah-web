# LED Downlights Flickering or Dying Early: What Is Really Wrong

**Question:** My LED downlights flicker, dim intermittently, or stop working within a year or two. Why do they fail so fast when LEDs are supposed to last for years?

**Answer:** In almost every case the LED chip is not the part that failed. The four real causes are a low-quality LED driver (the small electronic ballast behind each light), a dimmer switch that is not LED-compatible, a loose connection at the ceiling junction, and heat build-up where a downlight is enclosed without ventilation. Voltage fluctuation can contribute. Replacing the same cheap fitting again produces the same result within months — the correct fix is to identify which of the four applies and address that.

Flickering is worth taking seriously for one reason beyond irritation: a loose connection generates heat, and loose connections in ceiling voids are a recognised fire risk.

## Diagnose It by the Pattern

| What you observe | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| One light flickers, others on the same circuit are fine | That fitting's driver is failing |
| All lights on a dimmer flicker, worst at low brightness | Dimmer not LED-compatible, or below its minimum load |
| Lights dim briefly when the air-conditioner or kettle starts | Voltage drop on a shared or undersized circuit |
| Flicker is random across several rooms | Loose connection in the DB or a junction box |
| Light works then cuts out after 20–30 minutes, returns when cool | Heat build-up; the driver is thermally shutting down |
| Buzzing along with flicker | Driver and dimmer incompatibility |
| Flicker plus a burning smell | Stop using the circuit and call an electrician immediately |

## The Four Real Causes

### 1. Cheap or Failing LED Drivers
Every LED downlight contains a driver that converts mains AC to the low-voltage DC the LED chips need. The chips themselves can last many thousands of hours, but a low-quality driver uses electrolytic capacitors that degrade quickly in a hot ceiling void. This is why unbranded downlights fail long before their advertised life.

This is also why "the bulb died" is usually the wrong description — the electronics died, and on integrated fittings there is no separate bulb to change.

### 2. Incompatible Dimmer Switches
A dimmer designed for old incandescent bulbs works by chopping the AC waveform. LED drivers often cannot handle that chopped waveform smoothly, producing flicker, buzzing or a dead band at low brightness. Many dimmers also have a minimum load — replacing eight 50 W halogens with eight 7 W LEDs drops the load below what the dimmer needs to operate correctly.

The fix is a genuine LED-compatible (trailing-edge) dimmer matched to the fittings, not a different LED.

### 3. Loose Connections
Downlights are connected in ceiling voids, often with push-fit connectors. Thermal cycling, vibration from ceiling fans, and poor original workmanship all loosen these over time. A loose connection produces intermittent flicker, localised heating, and eventually a failed circuit. This is the cause that has a genuine safety dimension.

### 4. Heat and Enclosure
A downlight sitting directly against insulation, in a sealed ceiling pocket, or in a fitting with no ventilation runs hot. Heat is the primary killer of LED drivers. A downlight rated for enclosed installation is built differently from a standard one — a distinction usually ignored when fittings are bought on price alone.

## What a Proper Repair Involves

1. **Establish the pattern** — one fitting, one circuit, or the whole house.
2. **Check the connections** at the fitting and at the junction, tighten and insulate correctly.
3. **Test dimmer compatibility** where dimmers are involved, and confirm the load is within the dimmer's range.
4. **Replace failed fittings** with a quality branded LED downlight — a driver from a reputable manufacturer is what actually determines lifespan.
5. **Check ventilation and clearance** so the replacement is not installed in the same thermal trap.
6. **Investigate supply-side issues** where dimming coincides with heavy appliances starting — that points to circuit loading rather than the lights.

## Choosing Downlights That Last

- Buy on driver quality, not just lumens and price. Reputable brands publish real warranty terms.
- Match the dimmer to the fitting, and confirm the total load falls within the dimmer's stated range.
- Check whether the fitting is rated for enclosed or insulation-contact installation if your ceiling void is tight.
- Prefer replaceable-lamp fittings where practical — with an integrated unit, a driver failure means replacing the whole fitting.
- Keep colour temperature consistent across a room; mixing 3000K and 4000K in one ceiling looks like a fault even when nothing is wrong.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are all LED downlights the same?
No. The visible part looks similar, but driver quality varies enormously and that is what determines lifespan. Cheap unbranded fittings commonly fail long before branded ones.

### Can I just replace the bulb myself?
If the fitting takes a separate replaceable lamp, yes. Integrated LED downlights are hard-wired into the ceiling and should be replaced by a licensed electrician.

### Why do my LEDs flicker only when dimmed?
Because the dimmer is not LED-compatible, or the total LED load is below the dimmer's minimum. Changing to an LED-compatible dimmer usually resolves it.

### Is flickering dangerous?
It can be. Flicker caused by a loose connection generates heat in the ceiling void, which is a fire risk. Flicker with any burning smell or warm switch plate should be treated as urgent.

### My LEDs turn off after half an hour and come back later. Why?
That is thermal shutdown. The driver is overheating in an enclosed or unventilated position and protecting itself.

### How much to replace downlights across a room?
Branded supply and install is published at RM 120 to RM 220 per point, so a room's total depends on the number of points and whether any rewiring is needed.
